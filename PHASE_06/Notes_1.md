## **1. Introduction to NLP**

### **English Definition**

Natural Language Processing (NLP) is a branch of Artificial Intelligence (AI) that enables computers to understand, analyze, interpret, and generate human languages such as English, Hindi, and other languages.

### **Hinglish Explanation**

NLP Artificial Intelligence ki ek branch hai jo computers ko human language samajhne, analyze karne aur uska response generate karne mein help karti hai.

Normally, humans computer se English, Hindi ya voice mein baat karte hain. Computer ko human language directly samajhna difficult hota hai. NLP computer ko human language samajhne ki ability provide karta hai.

### **Example**

Hum Google Assistant se bolte hain:

"Hey Google, weather batao."

Google hamari language ko understand karke weather information provide karta hai.

Ye NLP ka example hai.

## **Step 1: Lexical Analysis**

English: Lexical Analysis divides text into meaningful words or tokens.

Hinglish: Is step mein sentence ko words aur tokens mein divide kiya jaata hai.

Example:

`I love AI.`

Tokens:

`I | love | AI | .`

## **Step 2: Syntactic Analysis (Parsing)**

English: Syntactic Analysis checks grammatical structure and relationships between words.

Hinglish: Is step mein check hota hai ki sentence grammar ke according correct hai ya nahi.

Example:

- Correct: Ram eats an apple.
- Incorrect: Apple an eats Ram.

## **Step 3: Semantic Analysis**

English: Semantic Analysis determines the literal meaning of words and sentences.

Hinglish: Is step mein sentence ka actual meaning check kiya jaata hai.

Example:

"The boy eats an apple."

Meaning logically correct hai.

"The boy eats a chair."

Grammar correct ho sakti hai, lekin meaning unusual hai.

## **Step 4: Discourse Integration**

English: Discourse Integration understands a sentence using the context of previous sentences.

Hinglish: Ismein previous sentence ki help se next sentence ka meaning samjha jaata hai.

Example:

"Ram bought a car. He drives it daily."

Yahan:

- He = Ram
- It = Car

## **Step 5: Pragmatic Analysis**

English: Pragmatic Analysis identifies the intended meaning of a sentence using real-world knowledge and context.

Hinglish: Ismein sentence ka actual intention samjha jaata hai, sirf literal meaning nahi.

Example:

"Can you open the door?"

Literal meaning: Kya tum door open kar sakte ho?

Actual intention: Please door open kar do.

| Discourse | Previous sentences se context lena |

# **5. Finding the Structure of Words**

## **5.1 Words and Their Components**

Words are made up of smaller meaningful units called morphemes.

Example:

`Unfriendly = Un + Friend + ly`

- Un = Negative meaning
- Friend = Main meaning
- ly = Word formation suffix

Hinglish: Ek word ke andar chhote meaningful parts ho sakte hain. Un parts ko morphemes kehte hain.

## **5.2 Lexemes**

English: A lexeme is an abstract vocabulary unit that represents different grammatical forms of a word.

Hinglish: Lexeme ek basic vocabulary concept hota hai jo word ki different forms ko represent karta hai.

Example:

- Play
- Plays
- Played
- Playing

Ye sab ek hi lexeme PLAY ki forms hain.

## **5.3 Morphemes**

English: A morpheme is the smallest meaningful unit of a word.

Hinglish: Morpheme word ka sabse chhota meaningful part hota hai.

Examples:

1\. Cats

Cat

s

Cat = Animal | s = Plural

2\. Played

Play

ed

Play = Action | ed = Past tense

3\. Unhappy

Un

Happy

Un = Not | Happy = Feeling

## **5.4 Morphology**

English: Morphology is the study of the internal structure and formation of words.

Hinglish: Morphology mein hum study karte hain ki words kaise bante hain aur unke parts kya hain.

Example:

`Replayed = Re + Play + ed`

- Re = Again
- Play = Main word
- ed = Past tense

## **5.5 Stems and Affixes**

**Stem:** Main part of a word.

**Affix:** Additional part attached to a stem.

Example:

`Unhappiness = Un + Happy + Ness`

- Happy = Stem
- Un = Prefix
- Ness = Suffix

## **5.6 Stemming**

English: Stemming is the process of removing affixes from words to obtain their stem.

Hinglish: Stemming mein word ke ending ya beginning ke extra parts remove karke root-like form nikala jaata hai.

Morphological Typology languages ko word formation ke basis par classify karti hai.

## **Main Types**

### **1. Analytic or Isolating Languages**

Words generally contain fewer morphemes.

Example: Chinese, Vietnamese.

### **2. Synthetic Languages**

Words mein multiple morphemes combine ho sakte hain.

Synthetic languages ke 2 main types:

- Agglutinative
- Fusional

### **3. Agglutinative Languages**

Har morpheme usually ek specific function convey karta hai.

Examples: Japanese, Korean, Tamil.

### **4. Fusional Languages**

Ek morpheme multiple grammatical meanings express kar sakta hai.

Examples: Sanskrit, Latin, Arabic, German.

| Analytic      | Separate words                             |

## **7.1 Tokenization**

English: Tokenization is the process of dividing text into smaller units called tokens.

Hinglish: Tokenization mein sentence ya paragraph ko chhote pieces mein divide kiya jaata hai.

### **Types of Tokenization**

**Word Tokenization**

Sentence ko words mein divide karna.

Input:

`I love Natural Language Processing.`

Output:

`I | love | Natural | Language | Processing | .`

**Sentence Tokenization**

Paragraph ko sentences mein divide karna.

Input:

`I am learning NLP. NLP is interesting.`

Output:

- I am learning NLP.
- NLP is interesting.

### **Python Program**

```
from nltk.tokenize import word_tokenizetext = "I love NLP"words = word_tokenize(text)print(words)
```

Output:

```
['I', 'love', 'NLP']
```

## **7.2 Stop Words Filtering**

English: Stop words are commonly occurring words that may be removed during text preprocessing when they are not useful for a particular task.

Hinglish: Stop words aise common words hote hain jo text analysis mein kabhi-kabhi remove kiye jaate hain.

Examples:

- is
- am
- the
- an
- in
- of

Input:

`I am learning NLP`

After filtering:

### **Python Program**

```
from nltk.corpus import stopwordsfrom nltk.tokenize import word_tokenizetext = "I am learning NLP"words = word_tokenize(text)stop_words = set(stopwords.words("english"))result = []for word in words:    if word.lower() not in stop_words:        result.append(word)print(result)
```

Output:

```
['learning', 'NLP']
```

Important: Stop words har application mein remove nahi karne chahiye. For example, sentiment analysis mein "not" important ho sakta hai.

## **7.4 POS Tagging**

POS stands for Parts of Speech.

English: POS Tagging assigns grammatical labels to words.

Hinglish: POS Tagging mein har word ko uske grammatical role ke according tag diya jaata hai.

Example:

`Ram eats an apple.`

| Word  | POS        |
| ----- | ---------- |
| Ram   | Noun       |
| eats  | Verb       |
| an    | Determiner |
| apple | Noun       |

### **Important POS Categories**

| POS          | Meaning                  | Example   |
| ------------ | ------------------------ | --------- |
| Noun         | Person/place/thing       | Ram       |
| Pronoun      | Noun ki jagah            | He        |
| Verb         | Action                   | Eat       |
| Adjective    | Noun ki quality          | Beautiful |
| Adverb       | Verb/adjective ki detail | Quickly   |
| Preposition  | Relationship             | In        |
| Conjunction  | Connects words           | And       |
| Interjection | Emotion                  | Wow!      |

### **Python Program**

```
import nltkfrom nltk.tokenize import word_tokenizetext = "Ram eats an apple"words = word_tokenize(text)print(nltk.pos_tag(words))
```

Output format:

```
[('Ram', 'NNP'), ('eats', 'VBZ'), ('an', 'DT'), ('apple', 'NN')]
```

## **7.6 Chunking**

English: Chunking groups words into meaningful phrases using POS tags.

Hinglish: Chunking mein POS tags ki help se words ko groups ya phrases mein arrange kiya jaata hai.

Example:

`A beautiful flower`

This can form a Noun Phrase (NP).

Noun Phrase

A

Beautiful

Flower

Determiner + Adjective + Noun

### **Python Program**

```
import nltksentence = "A beautiful flower"words = nltk.word_tokenize(sentence)tags = nltk.pos_tag(words)grammar = "NP: {<DT>?<JJ>*<NN>}"parser = nltk.RegexpParser(grammar)tree = parser.parse(tags)print(tree)
```

## **7.9 TF-IDF**

TF-IDF stands for:

**Term Frequency – Inverse Document Frequency**

English: TF-IDF is a statistical technique used to measure the importance of a word in a document relative to a collection of documents.

Hinglish: TF-IDF calculate karta hai ki koi word ek document mein kitna important hai, compared to complete document collection.

### **Two Components**

**1. TF – Term Frequency**

Term Frequency measures how frequently a word occurs in a document.

Formula:

\\[ TF=\frac{\text{Number of times term occurs}}{\text{Total terms in document}} \\]

Example:

Document contains 100 words.

The word "AI" appears 20 times.

\\[ TF=\frac{20}{100}=0.2 \\]

**2. IDF – Inverse Document Frequency**

IDF measures how rare or common a word is across all documents.

Formula:

\\[ IDF=\log\left(\frac{\text{Total documents}}{\text{Documents containing term}}\right) \\]

Example:

- Total documents = 10,000
- Documents containing AI = 100

\\[ IDF=\log(10000/100)=2 \\]

**3. TF-IDF Formula**

\\[ TF\text{-}IDF=TF\times IDF \\]

\\[ =0.2\times2=0.4 \\]

### **Easy Understanding**

- Word frequently appears in a document → TF high.
- Word appears in very few documents → IDF high.
- Both values combine → TF-IDF score.

**Real-life use:** Search engines, text classification, document retrieval, spam detection.
